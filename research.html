Research

Six manuscripts on keeping a school powered when winter takes the grid away.

Where code and data are archived, they are linked, so the work can be checked or applied to a different school.

New York State — schools as winter shelters

  • 1. Can an electrified school stay open in a blizzard? Solar-plus-storage sizing for winter shelter operation under cold-battery and snow constraints in New York State

    How much solar and storage a school needs to run as a shelter through a blizzard, once the cold has taken part of the battery and snow part of the array.

    Code and data: doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23931647

  • 2. Charge before the storm: forecast-informed battery readiness for school winter shelters in New York State

    Whether using the weather forecast to decide how full the battery should be before a storm changes how long the shelter can stay open.

    Code and data: doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23905411

  • 3. Cold-Morning Peaks from Electrified School Heating in New York State: Heat-Pump Choice, Weather-Year Risk and Short-Duration Storage

    What electrifying a school’s heating does to its coldest-morning demand, and how much of that peak a few hours of storage can absorb.

    Code: github.com/iamshokir/school-peak-ny

Tajikistan — mountain school microgrids

  • 4. Thermal siting and battery viability in cold-climate PV–battery–diesel microgrids: a scenario-robust sizing study for a remote mountain school in Tajikistan

    Where the battery should sit, and whether it stays worth installing at all, tested across many scenarios rather than one convenient design year.

    Code and data: doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23283637

  • 5. Warm room, insulated box or heater? Life-cycle thermal management of LFP batteries in off-grid mountain school microgrids

    Three ways to keep a lithium-iron-phosphate battery warm enough to work in a mountain winter, compared over the life of the battery.

Three countries — what happens next

  • 6. When the Main Grid Arrives: Institutional Coordination for Decentralised Electricity Systems in Nepal, Uttar Pradesh, and Tajikistan

    What becomes of a decentralised system once the national grid reaches it, and which institutions have to agree for that transition to go well.

Questions about the method or the data are welcome: shokir@oftoenergy.org.
