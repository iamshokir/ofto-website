Mission

Keeping schools powered through the coldest days of the year.

OFTO Energy Foundation studies how a school can keep its lights, heat and computers running on its own power when the grid fails in winter — and publishes the models and data so anyone can use them.

What we do

Since 2025 the initiative has worked on one question: how to size solar generation, battery storage and microgrids for a school building that has to work in the cold, and what happens to a battery that is asked for its deepest discharge on its coldest morning.

The work is carried out by volunteer researchers and written up as journal and conference manuscripts, with archived code and data wherever possible.

Two settings, one question

Tajikistan. Mountain schools where winter supply has been short for decades. Roughly 70% of the country’s population goes short of electricity in winter (World Bank, 2013, Tajikistan’s Winter Energy Crisis). A school may have to run on whatever it can generate on site.

New York State. Schools that open as community shelters during outages, which means they must hold heat and power on exactly the days the grid is least dependable.

Both share the same physics and the same stake: cold batteries, a winter peak, and a building the whole community falls back on. That is why one method travels between them.

Our first pilot

We are preparing our first pilot: a solar-plus-storage system for a mountain school in Tajikistan, designed with the methods described on the Research page.
